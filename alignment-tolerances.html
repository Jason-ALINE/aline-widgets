Alignment Tolerances

An alignment tolerance defines the acceptable range of misalignment that does not affect a machine's ability to rotate properly.

Definition

What is misalignment?

Misalignment is the deviation of relative shaft position from a collinear axis of rotation, measured at the points of power transmission, when the equipment is running at normal operating conditions.

General Purpose Equipment
3,600 RPM and below
Rule of Thumb
.0005" per inch of coupling span
.0005" (½ thousandth) per inch of distance between the flex planes is a reliable starting tolerance for most general purpose rotating equipment.

Other Factors

Beyond speed and span

The .0005"/inch rule uses only speed and coupling span. When setting tighter tolerances, also consider: how critical the equipment is to your operation, mechanical stiffness of the machine train, excitation forces influencing rotor dynamics, and thermal growth expectations.

When in doubt, align tighter. Tighter tolerances give a margin of safety against unpredictable factors.
