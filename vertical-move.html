Vertical Move, Step by Step

This is the up-and-down half of a reverse-indicator alignment — the one where you'll actually add or remove shims. Work through these steps in order, even if you've already run the Horizontal Move.

Haven't measured your bracket distances yet? Set up and measure A, B, and C on the Introduction page first — this guide picks up right after that.

Before You Start
  • A soft foot check already done on the machine
  • A sag check already done on your bracket setup
  • Square grid graph paper and a pencil
  • Both brackets mounted, bridging the coupling
  • Indicator stems aimed straight at the opposing centerline
  • Shim stock and wrenches for the hold-down bolts
The reverse-indicator method, viewed from the side — the same mirrored sign convention drawn in the original 1985 patent illustration.
1
Step 1 of 10
Compensate for Sag

A long indicator holder bends slightly under its own weight, which adds a small false reading even when nothing's misaligned. That false reading is called sag. If your sag check found, say, .002" of sag, start both indicators at +2 instead of zero before you begin — this cancels it out.

Without compensation, sag reads as false misalignment.
Haven't run a sag check yet? Do that first — it tells you the exact starting number to dial in here.
2
Step 2 of 10
Zero on Top

With both indicator feelers touching the top of the opposing bracket's arc, loosen each bezel and rotate the dial until the needle reads 0 — or your sag-compensated starting number from Step 2. Lock the bezel without bumping the needle.

Confirm both indicators are at the same starting point before rotating.
3
Step 3 of 10
Rotate to the Bottom

Both brackets bridge the coupling, so the shafts rotate together as one unit. Slowly turn the coupling 180° until the feelers — which were on top — now rest on the bottom of the opposing bracket's arc.

Watch for: a sticking or jumping needle. If the motion isn't smooth, stop and check the feeler contact before reading.
4
Step 4 of 10
Read & Record

With the feelers now on the bottom, read each dial and write down the number — including its sign.

Pump indicator reads — −12
Motor indicator reads — +8
5
Step 5 of 10
Find Half of Each Reading

Just like the horizontal move, divide each number by two. This half-value — TIR/2, half the Total Indicator Reading — is the actual distance the centerline is off at that point.

−12 → −6
+8 → +4
6
Step 6 of 10
Plot the Points

On your graph paper — fixed machine on the left, moveable on the right, same as always — use your Step 1 distances to mark where each indicator sits. Plot a point at its half-reading: −6 under the pump indicator, +4 under the motor indicator.

Two points plotted — nothing connected yet.
7
Step 7 of 10
Draw the Line and Extend It

Connect your two points with a straight line, then extend that line past the motor's front and back feet. This line is where the moveable shaft's centerline actually sits, top to bottom, the full length of the machine.

The extended line now crosses both foot positions.
8
Step 8 of 10
Read Your Shim Adjustment

Count the squares from the centerline to where your line crosses each foot. Each square is .001" — that count is exactly how much shim to add or remove at that foot.

Front foot — add .003" shim
Back foot — add .001" shim
9
Step 9 of 10
Add or Remove Shims

Loosen the hold-down bolt at each foot just enough to lift it slightly. Slide in (or remove) shim stock to the thickness from Step 9, then snug the bolt back down without fully torquing it yet.

A positive reading at a foot means that foot needs more shim under it (it needs to rise). A negative reading means remove shim (it needs to drop).
10
Step 10 of 10
Recheck

Zero both indicators on top again (compensating for sag if needed), rotate 180° to the bottom, and read. If both land at — or close to — zero, the vertical move is done. If not, repeat Steps 7–10 with the new readings — it should be a much smaller correction this time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the sag check and zeroing at a plain 0 when the holder actually sags — every reading after that is off.
  • Reading an indicator before confirming the feeler is making solid contact with the bracket surface.
  • Mixing up which foot is "front" and which is "back" between measuring and shimming.
  • Stacking too many thin shims instead of using the fewest pieces that add up to the right thickness.
  • Fully torquing the hold-down bolts before rechecking — always recheck before final torque.
