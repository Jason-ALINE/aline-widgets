Horizontal Move, Step by Step

This is the side-to-side half of a reverse-indicator alignment. If you've never run this procedure before, follow these steps in order — each one builds on the last, and nothing here assumes you've done it before.

Haven't measured your bracket distances yet? Set up and measure A, B, and C on the Introduction page first — this guide picks up right after that.

Before You Start
  • Wrenches to loosen the moveable machine's hold-down bolts
  • A soft foot check already done on the machine
  • A sag check already done on your bracket setup
  • Square grid graph paper and a pencil
  • Both brackets mounted, bridging the coupling
  • Indicator stems aimed straight at the opposing centerline
The reverse-indicator method, viewed from directly overhead — the same mirrored sign convention drawn in the original 1985 patent illustration.
1
Step 1 of 10
Get Your Bearings

Stand at the pump end, facing the coupling toward the moveable machine. From this position, identify left and right. Always view the pump from this same direction for the rest of this move. If you switch which end you're standing at halfway through, your plus and minus signs stop meaning anything.

In plain terms: stand at the pump and look down the shafts toward the motor. Left and right from that one fixed viewpoint — don't walk around to the other side partway through.
Top-down view: stand at the pump, look toward the motor — left and right follow your own view.
2
Step 2 of 10
Zero Both Indicators

With both indicator feelers touching the left side of the opposing bracket, loosen each indicator's bezel and rotate the dial face until the needle lines up with 0. Lock the bezel back down without bumping the needle off zero.

Both indicators should rest exactly on 0, feelers on the left, before you rotate.
3
Step 3 of 10
Rotate the Shafts Together

Because both brackets are bridging the coupling, the shafts rotate together as one unit. Slowly turn the coupling 180°, until the indicator feelers — which were on the left — now rest on the right side of the opposing bracket.

Watch for: a needle that sticks, jumps, or doesn't move smoothly. If it does, stop and recheck that the feeler is making clean contact with the bracket surface before reading anything.
4
Step 4 of 10
Read & Record

With the indicators now resting on the right, read each dial and write down the number — including its sign. If the needle swung into the side of the dial marked +, record it as positive; if it swung into the − side, record it as negative.

Pump indicator reads — −8
Motor indicator reads — +10
5
Step 5 of 10
Find Half of Each Reading

Each indicator's full swing is twice the actual offset, because both shafts moved relative to each other. So divide each number by two — this half-value, called TIR/2 (half the Total Indicator Reading), is the real distance the centerline is off at that point.

TIR = Total Indicator Reading — the full number the gauge swings through. You always work with half of it.
−8 → −4
+10 → +5
6
Step 6 of 10
Plot the Points

On your graph paper, mark the fixed machine on the left and the moveable machine on the right — same as you'll always view it. Using the distances from Step 1, mark where each indicator sits, then plot a point at its half-reading: −4 under the pump indicator, +5 under the motor indicator.

Two points plotted — nothing connected yet.
7
Step 7 of 10
Draw the Line and Extend It

Lay a straight edge across your two points and draw a line connecting them. Keep going — extend that same line past the motor's front and back feet. This line represents where the moveable shaft's centerline actually sits, the full length of the machine.

The extended line now crosses both foot positions.
8
Step 8 of 10
Read Your Move at Each Foot

Count the squares from the centerline (the zero line) to where your drawn line crosses each foot position. That distance — read as inches on the horizontal scale of your graph — is exactly how far, and which direction, each foot needs to move.

Front foot — .006" left
Back foot — .007" left
9
Step 9 of 10
Make the Move

Loosen the moveable machine's hold-down bolts just enough to slide it. Walk the back foot .007" toward the marked direction first, then the front foot .006". Snug the bolts back down without fully tightening yet.

Don't have graph paper handy? Shortcut: after zeroing on the left and rotating to the right, turn each indicator's bezel back to half its reading. Then walk the back foot until its indicator reads zero, then the front foot — alternating between the two until both read zero.
10
Step 10 of 10
Recheck

Zero both indicators on the left again, rotate 180° back to the right, and read. If both indicators land at — or close to — zero, the horizontal move is done. If not, repeat Steps 7–10 with your new readings; it will be a much smaller move the second time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Switching which end you're standing at partway through — this flips your left/right and ruins the plot.
  • Reading an indicator before confirming the feeler is making solid contact with the bracket surface.
  • Forgetting to record the sign (+ or −) along with the number — the sign is half the information.
  • Plotting the full indicator reading instead of half of it.
  • Tightening the hold-down bolts fully before rechecking — always recheck before final torque.
