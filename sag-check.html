Sag Check

A sag check determines how much an indicator bracket deflects under its own weight at a given distance — and lets you correct for it before taking any alignment readings.

What It Is

Why sag matters

A long indicator holder bends slightly under its own weight — even when the shafts are perfectly aligned. Without correcting for this sag, every vertical reading will carry a false negative offset. The sag check measures exactly how much that deflection is so you can compensate before you start.

The bracket bends under its own weight — sag is always negative and must be compensated on the vertical move.
Procedure

How to perform a sag check

  • 1Clamp the brackets on a sturdy piece of pipe at the same distance apart they will be when mounted on the actual equipment.
  • 2Zero both indicators on top, then rotate to the bottom. The difference between the top and bottom reading is the sag.
  • 3Record the sag value. Sag will always show as a negative number.
Example: If sag is .002" — when zeroing your indicators on top for the vertical move, start the needle at +2 instead of zero. This pre-compensates so the sag cancels out in the final reading.
