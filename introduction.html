Introduction

The reverse dial indicator method is probably the most popular method of shaft alignment because the dial indicators measure the relative position of two shaft centerlines directly — making it easy to illustrate graphically.

Concepts

What is reverse alignment?

Reverse alignment is the measurement of the axis, or centerline, of one shaft to the relative position of the axis of an opposing shaft centerline. This measurement can be projected the full length of both shafts for proper positioning when you need to allow for thermal movement.

The measurement also shows the position of the shaft centerlines at the coupling flex planes, for the purpose of selecting an allowable tolerance. The centerline measurements are taken in both horizontal and vertical planes.

The Graphical Method

Learning how to graph plot

Graphical alignment shows the relative position of the two shaft centerlines on square grid graph paper. Always view the equipment the same way it appears on the graph plot — Fixed on the left, Moveable on the right — both vertically and horizontally.
