Soft Foot Check

A soft foot condition exists when one of the machine's feet does not sit flat on the base. It must be corrected before alignment — tightening the hold-down bolts over a soft foot distorts the machine casing and invalidates every reading.

Types of Soft Foot

Two forms of soft foot

Angular Soft Foot
One corner of the foot is elevated — the foot only contacts the base on part of its surface and rocks when the bolt is tightened.
Parallel Soft Foot
The entire foot is elevated above the base by a uniform gap. Corrected by shimming with stock of even thickness under the full foot.
Angular soft foot — corner gap, foot rocks when bolted · Parallel soft foot — uniform gap across entire foot
Procedure

How to check for soft foot

  • 1Move indicators to the 12 o'clock position, depress the indicators and zero both.
  • 2Loosen one base bolt. If the indicator moves away from zero, slide shim stock under that foot — use the minimum thickness that will fit. Retighten the bolt and confirm the needle does not move.
  • 3Repeat for each remaining foot. Check all four feet before beginning alignment.
Important: Always correct soft foot before running a sag check or taking alignment readings. A machine that distorts under bolt load will never align repeatably.
